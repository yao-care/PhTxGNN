## Ipratropium: From Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease to Obstructive Lung Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ipratropium is an inhaled anticholinergic bronchodilator, originally used for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), allergic and vasomotor rhinitis, bronchitis and related airway conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, with **25 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this direction.
The predicted indication largely overlaps with its existing uses, so this is closer to confirming an established indication than to true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ipratropium is a muscarinic antagonist. It blocks M3 receptor-mediated bronchoconstriction in airway smooth muscle, which is the established mechanism of bronchodilation in obstructive airway disease. Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not currently available in the compiled data, so this mechanism is inferred from general pharmacology.

COPD, emphysema and bronchial disease are all forms of obstructive lung disease, so the prediction is mechanistically consistent with what the drug is already used for. Clinical evidence supports this, including a large Phase 3 trial of ipratropium/salbutamol in COPD and several Phase 3 comparisons against tiotropium.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions from the knowledge graph** (not label text): hypertension, long QT syndrome, epilepsy, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, benign prostatic hyperplasia, bladder neck obstruction, angle-closure glaucoma and ocular hypertension, hyperthyroidism, metabolic acidosis, potassium deficiency, myocardial ischemia and coronary artery disease, sudden cardiac arrest, and heart conduction disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found, and the 20 listed in the compiled data are all rated Moderate. Most involve other anticholinergic or antimuscarinic agents (e.g., atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, scopolamine, trospium, aclidinium), with additive anticholinergic effects likely. Others include promethazine, chlorpheniramine, prucalopride, eluxadoline and inhaled insulin.
- **Other signals**: Case reports describe severe anaphylaxis after ipratropium inhalation and unilateral mydriasis after inhalation in an infant.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Ipratropium has a well-established anticholinergic bronchodilator mechanism and large Phase 3 trial support in COPD, so evidence for obstructive lung disease is strong (L1). However, this largely confirms an existing indication, and its only Philippines registration is cancelled, so local availability and labelling must be resolved first.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarify the regulatory pathway, since the only registration (PNF-0259) is cancelled and has no recorded dosage form or indication text
- Obtain the package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Confirm the mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Set up a screening plan for patients with the cautions above (glaucoma, urinary obstruction, cardiac conditions) and for anticholinergic co-medication
