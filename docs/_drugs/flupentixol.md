## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, flupentixol is a thioxanthene antipsychotic that binds dopamine receptors (D1, D2, D3 and D5). Its efficacy in schizophrenia is linked to this dopamine antagonism.

A mechanistic link to retinal dystrophy has **not** been established. Retinal dystrophies are genetically diverse inherited conditions of photoreceptor or retinal pigment epithelium degeneration. There is no clear rationale for dopamine receptor antagonism to alter these processes. The very high score (99.99%) is a graph-based prediction only. It should not be read as evidence of benefit, especially since no trials or publications back it.

Other predictions in the list show the same pattern. These include Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1G, a congenital disorder of glycosylation, perisylvian polymicrogyria, hydranencephaly, several forms of myopia, and atypical glycine encephalopathy. All are at evidence level L5 with no mechanistic support. For the myopia predictions, retinal dopamine is thought to inhibit eye elongation, so a dopamine antagonist may be unfavourable rather than helpful.
