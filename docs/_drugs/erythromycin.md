## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, erythromycin is a macrolide that blocks bacterial protein synthesis at the 50S ribosomal subunit and also has anti-inflammatory activity. It is used topically in the eye for bacterial surface disease and blepharitis.

Punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis affects the ocular surface, where bacterial colonisation and inflammation of the lid margin and conjunctiva can play a part. This offers a plausible, but unproven, link: antibacterial and anti-inflammatory activity could help in related ocular surface disease.

The only retrieved paper is a review on managing chronic blepharokeratoconjunctivitis in children. It does not give direct efficacy data for this disease. The very high score is a model output and probably reflects closeness to existing conjunctivitis uses rather than proven benefit. Because no study directly addresses punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, the evidence level is L5.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in drug-disease records** (not label text): long QT syndrome (including familial form) and torsades de pointes; porphyria; kidney, liver, gallbladder and haematologic disease; *Clostridium difficile* colitis; exfoliative dermatitis and dermatitis; myasthenia gravis and other neuromuscular junction disease; folic acid deficiency anaemia; hearing disorders.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Major-level ones among those listed include **loperamide** and **clarithromycin**. Moderate-level ones include famotidine, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), pioglitazone, alosetron, aprepitant, chlorpropamide and cimetidine. Many of these are likely to be CYP3A4- or QT-related and should be checked before co-prescribing.

Please also refer to the package insert for full safety information.
