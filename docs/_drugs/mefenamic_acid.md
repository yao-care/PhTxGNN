## Mefenamic Acid: Headache disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Mefenamic acid is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) that blocks prostaglandin synthesis. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **headache disorder**. The data compiled for this report contain **no registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, including several small double-blind trials in migraine, a common headache disorder.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, mefenamic acid is an NSAID that inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX) and, through it, the synthesis and action of prostaglandins. Prostaglandins are involved in pain signalling and in some headache types, especially headaches linked to the menstrual cycle.

Because of this, the drug's analgesic action may be applicable to headache. The best-studied form of headache is migraine:
- A double-blind crossover trial compared mefenamic acid with paracetamol for acute migraine attacks.
- A double-blind crossover trial compared mefenamic acid with propranolol and placebo for migraine prevention.
- A double-blind placebo-controlled study tested it in menstrual migraine.

The evidence is concentrated in migraine and in menstrually related headache. Evidence for other headache types is thin or absent. Trigeminal autonomic headaches, for example, are generally not NSAID-responsive.

## Safety Considerations

- **Package insert:** The warnings and contraindications from the Philippine package insert were not available. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.
- **Drug Interactions:** The interaction check was completed with 100 interactions in total. Examples rated Moderate include:
  - corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone)
  - acetylsalicylic acid
  - metformin and chlorpropamide
  - mesalazine and balsalazide
  - aprepitant
  - potassium citrate and potassium bicarbonate

  Examples rated Minor include famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, dapagliflozin and linaclotide.
- **Conditions of concern (not label text):** The compiled data flag hypertension, kidney disease, liver disease, peptic ulcer disease, blood coagulation disorders, myocardial infarction and other cardiovascular disease.
- **Literature-reported risks:**
  - enteropathy with villous atrophy in prolonged use
  - analgesic-associated kidney damage
  - medication-overuse headache with frequent NSAID use

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Small, decades-old double-blind trials in migraine support the prediction, and the drug is marketed locally. However, the evidence covers only one headache type, no registered trials exist, and the package insert safety information is missing. Long-term or frequent use also carries gastrointestinal, renal and medication-overuse risks.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Obtain and review the Philippine package insert warnings and contraindications.
- Confirm the mechanism of action from a drug database.
- Define which headache types (for example menstrual migraine) are the target population.
- Obtain modern confirmatory trials, or a systematic review restricted to mefenamic acid.
- Set limits on treatment duration and frequency to avoid medication-overuse headache, with monitoring of gastrointestinal, renal and haematological safety.
