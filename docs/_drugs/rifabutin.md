## Rifabutin: From Mycobacterial Infection to HIV Infectious Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Rifabutin is a rifamycin-class antimycobacterial drug. Its original approved indication is not recorded in the Philippines license data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HIV infectious disease**, but the evidence supports HIV-associated mycobacterial disease (MAC and TB in people with HIV), not HIV infection itself.
The data compiled for this report include **33 clinical trials** and **20 publications** for this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Rifabutin is not an anti-HIV drug. It inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase (the rpoB target) and is used against mycobacteria. Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description relies on its known drug class.

Its link to HIV is through opportunistic infections. In people with advanced HIV, rifabutin is used to prevent and treat *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC) and to treat tuberculosis in patients taking antiretroviral therapy (ART). It is a weaker CYP3A4 inducer than rifampin, so it is often preferred when a patient takes protease inhibitors, although dose adjustment is still needed.

The high TxGNN score therefore reflects a real association, but the indication should be defined as **HIV-associated MAC/TB**. The evidence does not show that rifabutin treats HIV infection itself.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text): thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, and perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis.
- **Neutropenia**: Severe neutropenia led an earlier pediatric study with protease inhibitors to stop early. The 2019 and 2021 pediatric cohorts (PMIDs 31139825 and 33294914) also address this risk.
- **Uveitis**: Uveitis has been reported with rifabutin prophylaxis, particularly with itraconazole (PMID 8967681).
- **Drug interactions**: The interaction query returned 100 interactions. Among those listed, one is Major (eliglustat), and most of the rest are Moderate, including doxycycline, clarithromycin, metronidazole, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, morphine, aprepitant, pioglitazone, saxagliptin, linagliptin, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide, calcitriol, calcifediol, cholecalciferol and picosulfuric acid. Dolasetron is Minor. In HIV care, interactions with protease inhibitors, cabotegravir/rilpivirine, dolutegravir, maraviroc and other ART need dose adjustment or avoidance.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several Phase 3 randomized trials support rifabutin-containing regimens for MAC prevention and treatment in people with HIV, and TB/HIV pharmacokinetic data are substantial. However, the evidence supports HIV-associated mycobacterial disease, not treatment of HIV infection, and the Philippines registration is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Define the indication as HIV-associated MAC/TB, not HIV infection.
- Obtain the package insert warnings and contraindications.
- Confirm the original approved indication and the registration pathway, since the only license is cancelled.
- Prepare an interaction management plan for protease inhibitors, cabotegravir/rilpivirine, dolutegravir and other ART.
- Plan monitoring for neutropenia and uveitis, with particular care in children.
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank.

**Other predicted indications:** Leprosy (L4) is a research question, based on preclinical data, one in-silico assessment and a case report. Endocarditis and pneumocystosis (L4) rest on indirect case reports and co-occurrence in HIV literature, so they should be held. The remaining predictions (multiple endocrine neoplasia, sclerosing cholangitis, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder, conjunctivitis, feline AIDS and simian immunodeficiency virus infection) have no supporting trials or literature and should be held.
