## One-Sentence Summary

Rifabutin is an antimycobacterial antibiotic that inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **HIV infectious disease**, but the studies compiled for this report address tuberculosis and Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) infection in people with HIV, or interactions with antiretroviral drugs, rather than treatment of HIV infection itself. The evidence level for this prediction is therefore **L5**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, rifabutin is a rifamycin that inhibits the bacterial RNA polymerase (rpoB). It is not an anti-HIV agent and has no antiretroviral activity.

Its link to HIV is indirect. In people with HIV, rifabutin is used for opportunistic mycobacterial disease, namely MAC prophylaxis and treatment and tuberculosis in patients on antiretroviral therapy. It is a weaker CYP3A4 inducer than rifampin, which makes it preferable alongside protease inhibitors, although dose adjustment is still required. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects this frequent co-occurrence with HIV, not an effect on the virus. The evidence supports HIV-associated mycobacterial disease, not HIV infection itself.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in the interaction database. Examples include a major interaction with eliglustat and moderate interactions with clarithromycin, metronidazole, doxycycline, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone and pioglitazone. Interactions with protease inhibitors, cabotegravir/rilpivirine and other antiretrovirals need dose management.
- **Literature signals**: Neutropenia has been reported in children given rifabutin with lopinavir/ritonavir, and uveitis has been reported with rifabutin prophylaxis.

For other safety information, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No cited study treats HIV infection itself. The evidence concerns HIV-associated opportunistic mycobacterial disease and antiretroviral drug interactions, so the evidence level for this prediction is L5 and it does not support rifabutin as an HIV therapy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clearly defined indication, such as HIV-associated MAC or tuberculosis, instead of HIV infection
- Local package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism-of-action data
- An interaction management plan for protease inhibitors, cabotegravir/rilpivirine and other antiretrovirals
- Monitoring for neutropenia and uveitis
