## Rilpivirine: From HIV Infection to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Rilpivirine is a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, rilpivirine is an NNRTI that inhibits HIV-1 reverse transcriptase. Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) has a structurally related reverse transcriptase, so a mechanistic rationale exists.

However, the rationale is weak. NNRTIs are generally reported to have little or no activity against FIV reverse transcriptase because the NNRTI binding pocket differs. The only supporting study (2023) is a biochemical and structural comparison of NNRTIs against feline and human enzymes. It is in vitro-level evidence, and there is no in vivo or clinical data in cats. The very high TxGNN score is likely driven by proximity to HIV in the knowledge graph rather than by validated activity against FIV.

Note that this is a veterinary indication, not a human one.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence for feline AIDS is a single in vitro/structural study, and NNRTIs are generally reported to be weakly active against FIV. The high TxGNN score appears to reflect HIV network proximity. The only Philippine registration is cancelled, and the indication is veterinary.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from DOH-FDA Philippines
- In vitro antiviral activity data of rilpivirine against FIV, followed by in vivo studies in cats
- Clarification of the regulatory status of the cancelled registration (PNF-0424) and of any veterinary pathway
- Preclinical findings in macaques (SIV/SHIV) should be treated as supportive HIV pharmacology, not as a separate repurposing opportunity
