## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ketorolac is a non-selective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor. Blocking prostaglandin synthesis reduces peripheral and central sensitization, neurogenic inflammation and trigeminovascular activation, which drive migraine and tension-type headache pain.

Prostaglandin-mediated inflammation is also the target of ketorolac in its other anti-inflammatory uses, so the same mechanism may carry over to headache. In practice, parenteral ketorolac is already used in emergency departments for acute migraine and tension-type headache, and the trials and reviews below reflect this.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Examples include:
  - Major: acetylsalicylic acid
  - Moderate: dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, hydrocortisone, glipizide, glimepiride, glyburide, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide, exenatide, levofloxacin, kanamycin, balsalazide, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine
  - Minor: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, linaclotide
- **Other cautions from the literature**: a case report describes lithium toxicity when ketorolac was given with lithium. Typical NSAID risks (gastrointestinal bleeding, renal injury, cardiovascular events, platelet inhibition) apply, and parenteral use should be limited to a short course.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3/4 and Phase 2 trials and multiple systematic reviews and guidelines study ketorolac directly in migraine and tension-type headache, so the evidence is solid. However, many trials are small, use combination regimens or use ketorolac only as a comparator, and the safety profile of NSAIDs requires strict limits.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Philippine product, which are not available in the compiled data
- Clarification of the dosage form, route and approved indication of the local registration
- Mechanism of action data from a primary source
- A short-course dosing limit and screening for renal, gastrointestinal, bleeding and cardiovascular risk, and for interacting drugs such as lithium, anticoagulants and other NSAIDs
