# Clopidogrel: From Acute Coronary Syndrome to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Clopidogrel is an antiplatelet drug originally used for acute coronary syndrome, intracranial embolism and ST-elevation myocardial infarction.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, particularly migraine with a right-to-left shunt such as patent foramen ovale (PFO). This direction is supported by **6 clinical trials** (3 directly relevant to clopidogrel) and **20 publications** compiled for this report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known pharmacology, clopidogrel is a P2Y12 receptor antagonist that reduces platelet aggregation. Its efficacy in preventing thrombotic events in coronary and cerebrovascular disease has been established.

The proposed link to migraine is platelet activation and paradoxical microembolism, often through a right-to-left shunt such as PFO. These may trigger cortical spreading depression and aura. By blocking P2Y12, clopidogrel may reduce platelet aggregation and the release of platelet-derived serotonin and other vasoactive mediators. An animal study also reported involvement of P2Y12 receptors in a nitroglycerin-induced migraine model.

This rationale applies mainly to migraine in people with PFO or after septal defect closure. Evidence outside these settings is lacking. A randomised trial (COMPETE) and a 2026 BMJ publication of its results exist, but the findings were not available in the data compiled for this report, so their direction must be verified before any upgrade.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text)**: acute liver failure, hepatitis, blood coagulation disease, injury, thrombocytopenic purpura, active peptic ulcer disease, intracranial haemorrhage.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Major ones include:
  - Proton pump inhibitors: omeprazole, esomeprazole, rabeprazole
  - Pioglitazone
  - Loperamide
  - Repaglinide
  
  Moderate interactions include acetylsalicylic acid, morphine, bupropion, cimetidine, clarithromycin, lansoprazole and pantoprazole. Combining with other antithrombotics raises bleeding risk. Case reports describe intracerebral haemorrhage with concomitant celecoxib.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings, as these were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Randomised and observational data suggest clopidogrel may reduce migraine, mainly in patients with PFO or after septal defect closure. The key trials are small, pilot-sized or address a post-procedural setting. The pivotal COMPETE result was not available for verification. The only Philippines registration is cancelled, and label safety information is missing, so the safety screen cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Verify the COMPETE trial results (NCT05546320 and the 2026 BMJ publication), including the direction and size of effect and bleeding outcomes
- Obtain the Philippines package insert warnings and contraindications
- Obtain mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Confirm the regulatory pathway given the cancelled registration, and define the target population (migraine with PFO or right-to-left shunt)
- Prepare a bleeding-risk plan covering the listed contraindications and the major interactions, especially with proton pump inhibitors and other antithrombotics
