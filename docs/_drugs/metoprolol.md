## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, metoprolol is a beta-1 selective adrenergic blocker. It lowers heart rate and blood pressure and reduces renin release, and its efficacy in hypertension and myocardial infarction is established.

Malignant hypertensive renal disease is kidney injury caused by severely elevated blood pressure. Because it is essentially a severe, organ-damaging form of hypertension, the prediction is largely an extension of the existing hypertension indication. Reduced renin release and blood pressure control are plausible ways metoprolol could help. However, this link is a model prediction only. Beta-blockers are generally not first-line for hypertensive emergencies, so this should not be read as evidence of benefit.

Other predictions in the list fall into two groups:
- **Subtypes of existing indications** (not true repurposing): posterolateral, posteroinferior and septal myocardial infarction, and malignant renovascular hypertension.
- **Predictions with concerns or no support**: pulmonary hypertension (beta-blockade may be harmful), Braddock syndrome and trichotillomania (no identifiable mechanistic basis).

The best-supported prediction in terms of actual studies is **chronic pulmonary heart disease** (score 99.40%, evidence level L3). It is discussed below as a secondary research question.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text; to be verified against a package insert): anxiety and related disorders, pancreatitis, liver disease, gallbladder disease, systemic lupus erythematosus, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, hypotension, gout, hyperparathyroidism, hypercalcemia, hepatic coma and encephalopathy, anuria, myasthenia gravis and neuromuscular junction disease, psoriasis, pheochromocytoma, and several metabolic conditions (potassium deficiency, phosphorus metabolism disease, nephrocalcinosis).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found; most listed ones are Moderate. Examples include epinephrine, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), bupropion, morphine, cimetidine, SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin), chlorpropamide and calcium salts. Minor interactions include acetylsalicylic acid and magnesium oxide.
- **Respiratory safety**: In COPD, a terminated trial and later reports suggest metoprolol may increase exacerbations requiring hospitalization. Beta-blockade may also be harmful in pulmonary hypertension.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and label contraindications, which were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The predicted indication, malignant hypertensive renal disease, has only model-prediction support (L5) with no trials or literature. It is also largely an extension of the existing hypertension indication, and the only registration in the Philippines is cancelled.
- The only prediction with real study data (chronic pulmonary heart disease, L3) is a research question limited by respiratory safety concerns, not a ready candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from DOH-FDA Philippines (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clinical evidence for metoprolol in hypertensive renal injury, and a clinical rationale beyond existing antihypertensive options
- For chronic pulmonary heart disease: review of the BLOCK-COPD (NCT02587351) and BICS (PMID 38762800) safety signals, and results of NCT06825728 when available
- Clarification of the regulatory path, since no valid registration exists in the Philippines
