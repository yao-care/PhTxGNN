## Midazolam: From Anxiety to Insomnia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Midazolam is a short-acting benzodiazepine, originally used for anxiety.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Midazolam is a short-acting benzodiazepine. It enhances the inhibitory effect of GABA at GABA-A receptors, which produces sedative and hypnotic effects. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report, but this class-level mechanism is well characterised.

Anxiety and insomnia often occur together and share a common target: calming excessive neural arousal through GABAergic inhibition. Other benzodiazepines, such as flurazepam and triazolam, are established treatments for sleep disorders, so the prediction is pharmacologically plausible.

This is less a novel repurposing than a rediscovery. Oral midazolam was studied as a sleep medicine in the 1980s and 1990s, including a 14-day comparison with flurazepam in chronic insomnia. The very high TxGNN score is consistent with this history. However, the Philippines registration is cancelled, which limits practical translation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-related cautions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The TxGNN knowledge graph links midazolam with contraindications in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (including severe early-onset), cirrhosis of liver, angle-closure glaucoma, and chronic kidney disease or end-stage renal failure.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Examples at the **moderate** level include ranitidine, bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, omeprazole, cimetidine, clarithromycin, metoclopramide, miconazole and clotrimazole. Examples at the **minor** level include dexamethasone, betamethasone, magnesium oxide, aluminium hydroxide and calcium carbonate. Combining midazolam with other central nervous system depressants such as morphine deserves particular attention.

Please refer to the package insert for key warnings and labelled contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and older studies, including a randomised 14-day comparison with flurazepam, support midazolam as a hypnotic. However, both registered trials are indirect perioperative studies, the supporting data are decades old, and the Philippines registration is cancelled. The package insert safety review is also incomplete, so the candidate cannot yet advance past early screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Download and review the package insert warnings and contraindications from the DOH-FDA Philippines website
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A review of the 1980s–1990s oral midazolam insomnia studies, including dependence, next-day sedation and respiratory risks
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway, since the only registration is cancelled and the product's dosage forms are unknown
- A comparison with currently available hypnotics to establish whether there is a clinical need
