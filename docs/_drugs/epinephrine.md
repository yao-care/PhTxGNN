# Epinephrine: From Asthma and Anaphylaxis to Obstructive Lung Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Epinephrine is a non-selective adrenergic agonist, originally used for asthma, anaphylaxis, bronchitis, emphysema, bronchiectasis, hypotensive disorders and open-angle glaucoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease** (in practice, mainly infant bronchiolitis, since asthma is already an original indication), with **15 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the known pharmacology of epinephrine, it is a non-selective adrenergic agonist. Beta-2 receptor activation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle and causes bronchodilation, and alpha-1 activation reduces mucosal oedema.

This matches the original respiratory indications (asthma, bronchitis, emphysema, bronchiectasis). Obstructive lung disease covers asthma and COPD, so the predicted indication overlaps heavily with what the drug is already used for. The evidence for asthma is direct and strong, but it is **not a novel repurposing signal**.

The genuinely new question is **bronchiolitis in infants**. It is a related obstructive airway condition, but trial and meta-analysis results for epinephrine are inconsistent and show limited sustained benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not taken from label text): hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, atrial fibrillation, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest, cardiovascular disease, angle-closure glaucoma, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperthyroidism, liver and kidney disease, epilepsy, benign prostatic hyperplasia, myasthenia gravis, respiratory failure, and others.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified; the 20 reviewed here include **Major** interactions with the tricyclic antidepressants amitriptyline and amoxapine. Many **Moderate** interactions were also found, including beta-blockers (acebutolol, atenolol, betaxolol), other beta-agonists (formoterol, salbutamol, arformoterol), stimulants (amphetamine, methylphenidate, atomoxetine), antidiabetic agents (acarbose, alogliptin, albiglutide, acetohexamide) and antipsychotics (aripiprazole, asenapine, amisulpride).

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials support inhaled epinephrine in asthma, which gives a high evidence level. However, asthma is already an original indication, so this is not new. For the truly new use, infant bronchiolitis, the evidence is mixed, with limited sustained benefit. The Philippines registration is cancelled, so no product is currently marketed.

Other predicted indications are weaker:
- **Food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis**: Research question only (L4). It is a subtype of anaphylaxis, an original indication. There are no trials, only reviews and case reports.
- **Rienhoff syndrome**: Hold (L5). There is no trial or literature evidence and no clear mechanistic rationale.
- **Respiratory malformation**: Hold (L3). The only trial is a Phase 1 study in transient tachypnoea of the newborn, which is not a structural malformation, and the literature is mostly off-target.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the DOH-FDA Philippines (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A decision on whether to focus on infant bronchiolitis as the real repurposing question, with a review of the latest meta-analytic evidence
- Clarification of the cancelled registration status and the available dosage forms and routes in the Philippines
- A cardiovascular and drug-interaction safety plan, especially for patients on tricyclic antidepressants or beta-blockers
