# Mebendazole: From Enterobiasis, Ascariasis and Trichuriasis to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Mebendazole is an anthelmintic originally used to treat intestinal worm infections (enterobiasis, ascariasis and trichuriasis).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (an unrelated case report) behind this prediction, so it rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Mebendazole is generally known as an anthelmintic that binds tubulin and disrupts microtubule polymerization in parasites. This general knowledge is not part of the compiled data.

No established link was found between this mechanism and acne pathophysiology, such as sebum production, *C. acnes* colonization, or follicular inflammation. The original indications (intestinal worm infections) and acne are also unrelated diseases.

The very high score (0.992) is therefore treated as a computational prediction only. The biological rationale remains unsupported.
