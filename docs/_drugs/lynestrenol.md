## One-Sentence Summary

Lynestrenol is a progestin (a prodrug converted to norethisterone). The data compiled for this report do not specify its original approved indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, lynestrenol is a progestin that is converted in the body to norethisterone. It suppresses ovulation and stabilizes sex-hormone fluctuations.

Menstrual-related migraine is thought to be driven by the fall in estrogen before menstruation. A progestin that steadies hormone levels could plausibly reduce hormone-withdrawal-triggered attacks, which is the most likely basis for the high TxGNN score.

This reasoning should be treated cautiously. The only direct clinical evidence is a 1963 prophylaxis report, which was not designed to modern standards. The predictions for "migraine disorder" and "migraine with or without aura, susceptibility to" overlap and are one body of evidence, not independent support. The prediction for "migraine with brainstem aura" appears to inherit its score from the general migraine association, and no subtype-specific data exist for lynestrenol. The estrogen-containing contraceptives discussed in the 2026 review are generally avoided in migraine with aura because of stroke risk. A progestin-only agent is a different exposure, but this has not been studied for lynestrenol.

Other lower-ranked predictions were insomnia (99.08%) and leprosy (99.04%). Neither has trials or literature. The leprosy link has no plausible mechanism and is likely a model artifact.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The 2026 review notes that combined oral contraceptives are contraindicated in migraine with aura because of stroke risk. Whether this applies to a progestin-only agent such as lynestrenol has not been established.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but the supporting evidence is limited to a 1963 uncontrolled clinical report and a general review of contraceptive components. There are no registered trials, and the only Philippines registration is cancelled. Safety data, mechanism data and the original indication are all missing, so the case cannot responsibly advance beyond a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (from the DOH-FDA Philippines website), which are needed for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- Retrieval of the full text of the 1963 report, and a systematic search for modern studies of progestin-only treatment in menstrual-related migraine
- Clarification of the original approved indication and the reason for the registration cancellation
- Evaluation of the vascular and stroke risk of progestin-only exposure in patients with migraine, particularly migraine with aura
