## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, lynestrenol is a progestin prodrug that is converted in the body to norethisterone. It suppresses ovulation and can steady hormonal fluctuations.

Menstrual-related migraine is thought to be triggered by the fall in estrogen in the late luteal phase. A hormone-stabilizing drug could therefore plausibly reduce hormone-sensitive migraine, which is consistent with the high TxGNN score. This link is inferred. It has not been demonstrated for lynestrenol in modern studies.

A caution applies. Combined estrogen-containing contraceptives are contraindicated in migraine with aura because of stroke risk. A progestin-only agent may differ, but no data in this report address that question.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only study that directly addresses migraine with lynestrenol is a 1963 uncontrolled report without an available abstract. The other source is a general review of combined contraceptives. There are no registered trials, the mechanism is inferred, and no label or safety data were available. Because this evidence is thin, it supports further research only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieve the full text of the 1963 report and assess its design and results
- Mechanism of action data for lynestrenol and its active metabolite
- Package insert (indication, warnings, contraindications) from the DOH-FDA Philippines record
- Safety assessment of progestin-only use in migraine, especially migraine with aura
- A controlled study, or a systematic review of progestin-only treatment for menstrual-related migraine
