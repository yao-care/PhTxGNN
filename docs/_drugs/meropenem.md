## Meropenem: Bacterial arthritis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Meropenem is a broad-spectrum carbapenem antibiotic, originally used for serious bacterial infections such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection, peritonitis and bacterial meningitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bacterial arthritis**, but this top-ranked prediction currently has **0 clinical trials** and only literature made up of case reports, reviews, pharmacokinetic studies and resistance surveillance, so the evidence is weak.
Among the ten predictions compiled for this report, the best-supported one is **urinary tract infection** (9 trials and a set of RCTs), which largely confirms meropenem's established role rather than representing a new use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, meropenem is a carbapenem that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins. It has proven efficacy in serious Gram-negative and Gram-positive infections, and may mechanistically be applicable to bone and joint infections caused by susceptible organisms.

Bacterial arthritis (septic arthritis) is a bacterial infection, like the drug's original indications. The retrieved literature includes a case of ESBL-producing *Klebsiella pneumoniae* septic arthritis treated successfully with meropenem plus amikacin and arthroscopic washout, and a melioidosis series in which all isolates were susceptible to meropenem. However, the evidence is limited to case reports, a retrospective cohort, pharmacokinetic studies and resistance surveillance. No dedicated trials exist, so the link remains plausible but unproven.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the top-ranked prediction, bacterial arthritis)

**Rationale:**
- Bacterial arthritis has no clinical trials and only low-level evidence (case reports, a retrospective cohort, pharmacokinetic and surveillance studies), so it stays at L4.
- Complicated UTI is the only prediction with Phase 3 RCT support (L1, Proceed with Guardrails), but it confirms meropenem's established role rather than a new use. It would need stewardship guardrails (reserve for resistant or severe infections), and the Philippines registration is cancelled.
- *S. aureus* infection (L3) is a possible research question. Ureaplasma urethritis conflicts with known biology, and laryngitis, Lyme disease, Peptostreptococcus infection and gonococcal urethritis lack clinical support.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the DOH-FDA Philippines, which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- For bacterial arthritis: prospective or comparative clinical data on meropenem in septic arthritis and bone and joint infections
- Clarification of the cancelled registration status and whether re-registration or an alternative supply route is needed
- A stewardship and antimicrobial-resistance plan if any indication is pursued
