# Potassium: From an Unspecified Original Indication to Hypertensive Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Potassium is an essential mineral and electrolyte. In the Philippines it was registered as potassium phosphate, potassium chloride and potassium citrate, but all three registrations are now cancelled and no approved indication text is available.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **hypertensive disorder**, with **23 clinical trials** and **20 publications** retrieved. Most of the trials do not test potassium itself, and the strongest support comes from meta-analyses of dietary or supplemental potassium.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug in the data compiled for this report. The following reasoning is based on the physiology of potassium and on the published literature.

Higher potassium intake is thought to lower blood pressure through several routes:
- increased sodium excretion in the urine (natriuresis);
- relaxation of blood vessels, through potassium channels and the Na+/K+-ATPase pump in vascular smooth muscle;
- reduced sympathetic nervous tone.

Dose-response meta-analyses of randomized trials (PMID 32500831, 23558164) support a blood-pressure-lowering effect.

An important caveat is that this evidence comes mainly from dietary or supplemental potassium, not from a registered potassium drug product. Many of the retrieved trials involve losartan or mexartan potassium salts, where potassium is only the counter-ion, or other antihypertensive drugs. The most direct Phase 3 trials are NCT00000509 (results and enrollment not reported) and NCT03809884 (only 7 participants). This is why the evidence level is L2 and not L1.

The other four predicted indications have much weaker support:
- **Pulmonary hypertension, unclear multifactorial mechanism**, and **malignant hypertensive renal disease**: model prediction only, no trials or literature. For renal disease, potassium supplementation also carries a hyperkalemia risk.
- **Pulmonary hypertension owing to lung disease and/or hypoxia**: only basic science on oxygen-sensitive potassium channels. Raising extracellular potassium could, if anything, promote vasoconstriction.
- **Malignant renovascular hypertension**: only case reports and secondary hypertension literature, where low potassium is a consequence of the disease, not evidence that potassium treats it.

All four are rated L5 with a Hold recommendation.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report, and no drug interaction records were found. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

The main safety concern for potassium supplementation is hyperkalemia. Higher risk applies to:
- patients with chronic kidney disease or renal impairment;
- patients taking ACE inhibitors, ARBs or mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Meta-analyses of randomized trials support a blood-pressure-lowering effect of increased potassium intake, and the TxGNN score is high (99.16%). However, this evidence comes mainly from dietary or supplemental potassium. The Phase 3 trials that test potassium directly are small or lack reported results, and all Philippine registrations are cancelled. Evidence is therefore capped at L2.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert safety data (warnings, contraindications), which are currently missing and block formal safety screening
- Regulatory clarification of the cancelled market status and the missing original indication
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Results and enrollment details for NCT00000509, or a new adequately powered trial of a potassium product in hypertension
- A safety plan covering hyperkalemia: serum potassium and renal function monitoring, and exclusion or close monitoring of patients with CKD or on ACE inhibitors, ARBs or MRAs
