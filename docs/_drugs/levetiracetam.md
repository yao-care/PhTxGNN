# Levetiracetam: From Partial Epilepsy to Visual Epilepsy

## One-Sentence Summary

Levetiracetam is an antiseizure medicine, originally used to treat partial (focal) epilepsy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **visual epilepsy** (seizures triggered by visual stimuli), but there are **no registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, none of which studies this condition specifically.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Published literature describes levetiracetam as binding the synaptic vesicle protein SV2A and reducing neurotransmitter release. It is widely used as a broad-spectrum antiseizure drug. Its efficacy in partial epilepsy is established.

Visual epilepsy is a reflex (stimulus-triggered) seizure disorder. Seizures in these conditions arise from neuronal hyperexcitability, which is the process SV2A modulation is thought to dampen. So activity in photosensitive or visually triggered seizures is plausible.

However, the retrieved literature covers neonatal seizures, seizure prophylaxis after brain injury or stroke, post-stroke seizures, and feline epilepsy. It does not cover visually induced epilepsy. The very high TxGNN score is therefore not backed by disease-specific clinical data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is plausible mechanistically, but there are no trials and no disease-specific literature for visual epilepsy, so evidence is limited to model prediction and indirect antiseizure data (L4). The only registration in the Philippines is cancelled.

Among the other predicted indications, **status epilepticus** is the best supported, with Phase 3 RCTs (e.g., NCT01960075/ESETT). It is already an established use, so it is closer to confirming existing practice than repurposing. Startle epilepsy and reading epilepsy have small case-level evidence and could be treated as research questions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Disease-specific evidence for levetiracetam in visually induced or photosensitive epilepsy, such as photosensitivity (photoparoxysmal response) studies or case series
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the product
- Clarification of current Philippines regulatory status, since the sole registration is cancelled
- A safety monitoring plan for psychiatric, renal and haematological risks
