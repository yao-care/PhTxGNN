## Dexamethasone: From Adrenocortical Insufficiency and Inflammatory Conditions to Alopecia Areata
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Dexamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid originally used for conditions such as adrenocortical insufficiency, Crohn disease, acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and congenital adrenal hyperplasia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Alopecia Areata**.
No clinical trials are registered in the data compiled for this report, but **20 publications** (including one randomised controlled trial, a network meta-analysis and several cohort studies) point in this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, dexamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid that suppresses inflammatory and immune responses, and its efficacy in several inflammatory and immune-mediated conditions is well established.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on hair follicles, with collapse of the follicle's immune privilege. Dexamethasone suppresses cytotoxic T-cell activity and pro-inflammatory cytokine signalling, which gives a direct biological rationale. Oral mini-pulse dosing (for example, twice weekly) is an established off-label regimen described in the literature, and the high TxGNN score is consistent with the available clinical reports.

Other hair-related predictions (e.g. alopecia mucinosa, folliculitis decalvans, telogen effluvium) rank lower in evidence. Most have no supporting studies, and several are genetic or structural disorders for which a glucocorticoid mechanism is implausible.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from drug-disease links, not from label text): hypertension, congestive heart failure, hyperglycaemia, epilepsy, herpes simplex virus keratitis, measles, urea-cycle disorders (e.g. citrullinemia, hyperargininemia, carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency), and several pituitary hormone deficiency and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism conditions.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Examples include:

  - **Minor**: dolutegravir, lidocaine, formoterol

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One randomised trial, a network meta-analysis and several cohort studies support oral or IV dexamethasone pulse therapy in alopecia areata, and the mechanism is biologically plausible. However, there are no registered clinical trials, most studies are small or observational, and the Philippines registration is cancelled with no indication or label data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from DOH-FDA Philippines (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of a currently valid, marketed dexamethasone product and dosage form in the Philippines
- A safety monitoring plan for repeated pulse steroid use (blood pressure, glucose, infection risk, and growth in children)
- Positioning against JAK inhibitors and contact immunotherapy, given the comparative evidence
