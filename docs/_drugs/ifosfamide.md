## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, ifosfamide is a prodrug activated by liver cytochrome P450 enzymes (mainly CYP3A4 and CYP2B6) to isophosphoramide mustard. This active compound cross-links DNA and triggers cell death in proliferating tumour cells.

This cytotoxic mechanism is not specific to one tumour type, so activity in breast carcinoma is plausible. The compiled literature reports ifosfamide in advanced or metastatic breast cancer, including combinations with vinorelbine, etoposide, mitoxantrone, paclitaxel and docetaxel, and in metaplastic breast cancer. However, the evidence is mainly Phase 2 and single-arm studies. No Phase 3 randomised trial in breast cancer is present, and ifosfamide is not a standard-of-care agent in breast cancer.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Those listed are mostly of moderate level, including amphotericin B, bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, metoclopramide, scopolamine, dronabinol, nabilone, and aminoglycosides (kanamycin, neomycin). Several sulfonylureas and similar glucose-lowering agents (chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, tolazamide, acetohexamide, nateglinide, repaglinide) are also listed at moderate level. Levofloxacin is listed as minor.
- **Conditions of concern**: Conditions listed as areas of concern with ifosfamide include kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, anaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia, Gilbert syndrome, renal tubular acidosis, Fanconi syndrome and potassium deficiency. These are not taken from label text and must be checked against the package insert.
- **Class-related risks**: Nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity (encephalopathy), haemorrhagic cystitis (mesna is used for protection), myelosuppression and secondary malignancy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The compiled studies of ifosfamide in breast cancer are Phase 2 or smaller combination-regimen studies and older reviews. None is a randomised trial of ifosfamide alone in breast cancer, and its contribution within combinations is hard to separate. Package insert warnings are also not available, so safety screening cannot proceed. Because the studies address breast cancer directly but only at an early level of evidence, this remains a research question rather than a candidate for adoption.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Philippine regulator
- Mechanism of action data from a drug database
- Controlled trial data comparing ifosfamide-containing regimens with current standard breast cancer therapy
- A safety monitoring plan covering kidney, nervous system, bladder and bone marrow toxicity
