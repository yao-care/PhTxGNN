## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled data. The reasoning below rests on clozapine's known pharmacology, as described in the supporting literature.

Clozapine blocks dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, and it also acts on serotonergic, muscarinic, histaminergic and adrenergic receptors. This multi-receptor profile plausibly supports anti-manic and mood-stabilizing effects, particularly in mania that has not responded to conventional treatment. Many manic patients respond poorly to standard agents or cannot tolerate therapeutic doses, so an alternative with a different pharmacological profile is of clinical interest.

Clinical support comes from a completed double-blind Phase 2 trial in treatment-resistant mania and a 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis of clozapine in bipolar disorder. Most of this evidence comes from treatment-resistant populations. Any use should therefore be considered mainly in that setting.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
There is direct evidence for this indication: a completed Phase 2 double-blind trial (n=42) and a 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis. This corresponds to evidence level L2. The evidence is concentrated in treatment-resistant mania, and the trial is small and not Phase 3. Use should therefore be limited to carefully selected patients under strict safety monitoring.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Philippine-registered product
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- A hematologic monitoring plan (neutrophil counts) and cardiac, seizure and metabolic monitoring
- A review of the major and moderate drug interactions against likely co-medications in bipolar patients
- Larger controlled trials, or published results from ongoing trials, to confirm efficacy in mania
