## Clozapine: Manic bipolar affective disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clozapine is an atypical antipsychotic with a broad receptor profile (D2/5-HT2A antagonism plus serotonergic, muscarinic, histaminergic and adrenergic activity).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **manic bipolar affective disorder**, with **1 completed Phase 2 trial** (NCT00029458) and a **2020 systematic review and meta-analysis** supporting this direction. The evidence level is **L2**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Clozapine blocks dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors and also acts on serotonergic, muscarinic, histaminergic and adrenergic receptors. This broad profile plausibly supports anti-manic and mood-stabilizing effects, particularly in patients whose mania has not responded to conventional treatment.

Direct clinical support exists but is limited. A completed Phase 2 double-blind trial in treatment-resistant mania (n=42) and a 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis of three small comparative studies found clozapine similar to other antipsychotics in manic episodes and better in treatment-resistant bipolar disorder. There are no Phase 3 trials for mania itself, and the evidence is concentrated in treatment-resistant populations. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions of concern in clozapine safety data**: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, epilepsy, liver disease, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycemia, coronary artery disease, orthostatic and other hypotensive disorders, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, pulmonary embolism, respiratory failure, angle-closure glaucoma, paralytic ileus, benign prostatic hyperplasia, hyperlipidemia and obesity.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Major ones include bupropion, morphine and potassium citrate. Moderate ones include metformin, pioglitazone, famotidine, cimetidine, omeprazole, epinephrine and several anticholinergic agents (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium).
- **Literature signals**: mandatory haematological monitoring, cardiotoxicity (myocarditis, cardiomyopathy), seizure risk and metabolic syndrome are recurring concerns.

Please also refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2 double-blind trial and a 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis directly address clozapine in mania and treatment-resistant bipolar disorder, giving an L2 evidence level. The evidence is small and concentrated in treatment-resistant patients, and clozapine carries serious haematological, cardiac, seizure and metabolic risks.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Restriction of any use to treatment-resistant patients, with mandatory blood count monitoring
- Cardiac, seizure and metabolic monitoring plans
- A review of the interaction burden (100 recorded interactions) for co-prescribed medicines
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Philippines regulator
- Larger controlled trials specific to mania
- Detailed mechanism of action data
