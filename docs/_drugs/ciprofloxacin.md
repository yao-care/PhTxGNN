## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Ciprofloxacin is generally known to inhibit bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. This is an antibacterial mechanism and does not directly explain an effect on scleroderma.

Two possible links to diffuse scleroderma are suggested:

1. **Antifibrotic effect on skin.** A published study (PMID 20507401) examined oral ciprofloxacin as an antifibrotic in scleroderma skin. It may involve effects on collagen synthesis or fibroblasts, but this is a hypothesis. The abstract available here is truncated and does not give the results.
2. **Indirect gastrointestinal benefit.** Ciprofloxacin treats small bowel bacterial overgrowth, a common gastrointestinal complication of systemic sclerosis (PMID 7728404). This addresses a complication, not the underlying disease.

Neither link is supported by controlled clinical data in the material provided. The very high score is a computational prediction only.
