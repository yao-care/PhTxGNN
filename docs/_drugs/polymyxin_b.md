## One-Sentence Summary

Polymyxin B is a polypeptide antibiotic that acts against Gram-negative bacteria. Its original indication is not recorded in the Philippine registration data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **12 publications**, mostly preclinical, physiological or safety-related.
The evidence level is low (L4), so the recommendation is **Hold**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Polymyxin B is generally known as a cationic polypeptide antibiotic that disrupts the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria. There is therefore a plausible antibacterial rationale for bacterial bronchitis or tracheobronchitis caused by multidrug-resistant Gram-negative organisms such as *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Acinetobacter baumannii*.

However, the supporting literature is weaker than the score suggests:
- The clinical papers mostly concern ventilator-associated pneumonia, tracheobronchitis and salvage therapy with inhaled polymyxin B. They do not address bronchitis as a defined indication.
- Many other papers use polymyxin B as a tool to *induce* eosinophilic bronchitis in animal models, or as a bronchial provocation agent in people with asthma and chronic bronchitis. These do not support therapeutic benefit.
- The very high TxGNN score (0.9987) is a computational prediction only and is not independent clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query found 73 interacting drugs. Major-level interactions among those reviewed include:
  - Amikacin
  - Cisatracurium
  - Cidofovir
  - Deferasirox
  - Botulinum toxin type A
  - Botulinum neurotoxin A/B immune globulin
  - Intravenous human immunoglobulin G
  - Human cytomegalovirus immune globulin
  - Live oral cholera vaccine (*Vibrio cholerae* CVD 103-HgR)
  - Iodinated contrast agents (iothalamic acid, diatrizoate)

  Moderate-level interactions include ethinylestradiol, estradiol, mestranol, mesalazine, balsalazide, ibandronate, capreomycin, picosulfuric acid and clofarabine.
- **Airway delivery concern**: Several publications (1970–1985) show that inhaled polymyxin B can act as a non-specific bronchial provocation agent. It can cause bronchoconstriction in people with asthma and chronic bronchitis. This is a significant concern for any inhaled use in bronchitis.

Please refer to the package insert for further safety information, including warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- There are no clinical trials and no clinical evidence specific to bronchitis. Much of the literature points to a bronchoconstriction and airway-irritation risk with inhaled use. The high TxGNN score alone is not enough to advance.
- The only Philippine registration is cancelled, so no product is currently marketed.
- Another predicted indication, **conjunctivitis**, has stronger support: several randomized comparisons of topical polymyxin B/trimethoprim against moxifloxacin. This is closer to confirming an established use than to true repurposing. It may be worth evaluating separately, with guardrails.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings and contraindications from DOH-FDA Philippines
- Detailed mechanism of action data (e.g., from DrugBank)
- Clinical studies that specifically define bacterial bronchitis as the target, with a clear distinction from pneumonia
- A route and formulation assessment, including a safety evaluation of inhaled delivery given the bronchoconstriction reports
- Confirmation of local product availability, given that the registration is cancelled
