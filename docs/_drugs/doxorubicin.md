## Doxorubicin: From Hematologic Malignancies (Leukaemia and Lymphoma) to Ewing Sarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxorubicin is an anthracycline cytotoxic drug, originally used for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and related blood cancers.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ewing sarcoma**,
with **28 clinical trials** (including several Phase 3 trials) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Doxorubicin is a topoisomerase II inhibitor and DNA intercalator. It damages the DNA of rapidly dividing cells and is cytotoxic to fast-growing tumours. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description reflects the established pharmacology of the drug.

Ewing sarcoma is a fast-growing small round cell tumour of bone and soft tissue. Doxorubicin is already a backbone of the standard regimens used for it: VDC/IE (vincristine, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide, alternating with ifosfamide and etoposide). The TxGNN prediction is therefore consistent with the drug's known activity, and in practice the drug is already used this way. The main concern is cumulative cardiotoxicity, which is why the dexrazoxane cardioprotection trials matter.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindicated conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: cardiac conditions (heart failure, cardiomyopathy, heart disease, conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest), blood conditions (neutropenia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia), and liver conditions (liver failure, liver disease, hyperbilirubinaemia, Gilbert syndrome). Infectious disease and fungal infection are also listed, along with several other metabolic conditions.
- **Cumulative cardiotoxicity**: this is the main guardrail. The trial and literature record includes dexrazoxane cardioprotection studies and a review of anthracycline cardiotoxicity.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. The ones listed in the data include:
  - **Major**: dolasetron, cisapride, papaverine.
  - **Moderate**: famotidine, loperamide, aprepitant, dexamethasone, clarithromycin, granisetron, ondansetron, palonosetron, levofloxacin, rolapitant, eliglustat, metreleptin, troglitazone, and others.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Ewing sarcoma has multiple completed Phase 3 trials and RCT publications using doxorubicin-containing regimens, and doxorubicin is part of the standard care. Because those trials test the regimen as a whole, the evidence level is conservatively set at L2. The main risks are cumulative cardiotoxicity and myelosuppression. The Philippine registration is also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A valid Philippine registration or import pathway, since the only licence is cancelled
- A cardiac monitoring and dexrazoxane/cumulative-dose plan
- Review of the major and moderate drug interactions, especially QT-prolonging agents
