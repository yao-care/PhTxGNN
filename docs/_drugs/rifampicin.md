## Rifampicin: From Pulmonary Tuberculosis to Conjunctivitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Rifampicin is a broad-spectrum RNA polymerase inhibitor antibiotic, originally used to treat pulmonary tuberculosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **conjunctivitis**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **a small amount of weak literature** (mostly microbiology surveys, reviews and case reports) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, rifampicin is an antibiotic that inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase. It is active against Gram-positive bacteria, *Chlamydia* and some other intracellular organisms, and its efficacy in tuberculosis is well established.

This activity could plausibly apply to bacterial or chlamydial conjunctivitis. Older studies tested topical rifampicin ointment in trachoma and in chlamydial eye infection, which is the closest thing to direct clinical support. However, most of the retrieved literature consists of susceptibility surveys, case reports and general reviews, with no controlled data for rifampicin in conjunctivitis itself. Topical ophthalmic use is also not an established standard of care.

Other predictions were also reviewed. Most have even weaker support:
- **Rheumatoid arthritis** (score 0.9945, evidence level L3): several small, older clinical studies (1988–1993) showed limited benefit, and the idea was not adopted. The only registered trial is a Phase 1 drug-interaction study.
- **Acne**: the literature mostly concerns hidradenitis suppurativa (a different disease), where rifampicin plus clindamycin is used. There is no acne-specific trial.
- **HIV infection**: the trials concern HIV/TB co-infection, where rifampicin treats the TB. It is not an HIV treatment and does not count as repurposing.
- **Multiple endocrine neoplasia, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder, acute contagious conjunctivitis, simian immunodeficiency virus infection, feline AIDS**: computational prediction only, with no supporting evidence and no plausible mechanistic link.

## Safety Considerations

No label-based warnings or contraindications were available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Additional information from other sources:

- **Contraindication-related conditions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver failure and other liver disease, hepatitis, porphyria, blood disorders (thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, aplastic anaemia, anaemia), diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, peripheral neuropathy, gallbladder disease, substance abuse/dependence, slow acetylator status, and non-infectious meningitis.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were found, all or mostly rated Moderate in the visible list. Examples include metformin, pioglitazone, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, omeprazole, rabeprazole, bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, clarithromycin, doxycycline, metronidazole, and vitamin D forms (cholecalciferol, calcifediol, calcitriol). Rifampicin is a potent enzyme inducer, so co-medications need careful review.
- **Ocular toxicity**: two 1976 British Medical Journal reports describe ocular toxicity attributed to rifampicin, which deserves attention for any eye-directed use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but it is a model output only. There are no registered trials in conjunctivitis, and the literature is old, small or non-interventional. Rifampicin is marketed in the Philippines, with 1 valid licence of 1, but topical ophthalmic use is not standard care. None of the ten predicted indications is currently strong enough to advance, and rheumatoid arthritis has the most prior clinical testing but showed limited benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from DOH-FDA Philippines (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data (e.g. from DrugBank)
- A suitable topical or ophthalmic formulation and a registration pathway, since the only local licence is cancelled
- Modern controlled trial evidence for rifampicin in bacterial or chlamydial conjunctivitis, compared with current standard antibiotics
- A review of ocular safety, and of antimicrobial resistance concerns, since rifampicin is a key anti-tuberculosis drug
