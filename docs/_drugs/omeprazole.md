# Omeprazole: From Gastric Ulcer and Reflux Disease to Duodenogastric Reflux

## One-Sentence Summary

Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor, originally used for gastric and duodenal ulcers, gastroesophageal reflux disease, esophagitis and Zollinger-Ellison syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **duodenogastric reflux**, but the support is limited: **1 clinical trial** (a diagnostic imaging study, only indirectly relevant) and **15 publications** (mostly small physiological studies, animal studies and reviews).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor that suppresses gastric acid secretion. Its efficacy in ulcer disease and reflux disease is well established.

In duodenogastric reflux, bile and duodenal contents flow back into the stomach. Acid suppression may reduce the combined acid-and-bile injury to the mucosa and relieve symptoms. Small clinical studies in Barrett's oesophagus (PMID 10994616, 9824338) and a pediatric prospective study of oesophageal bile reflux (PMID 16641575) examined omeprazole in this setting.

Omeprazole does not bind bile or stop the reflux itself, so any benefit is likely symptomatic and indirect. Rat studies also raise a safety concern: acid blockade combined with duodenogastric reflux promoted mucosal growth stimulation and gastric carcinogenesis (PMID 10389684, 8943968). A later rat study (PMID 33027361) examined whether omeprazole might be protective. The link to the original indications is plausible, but the evidence is small, mostly physiological, and has mixed safety signals.

A second prediction, **duodenal obstruction** (score 99.64%, evidence level L4), was also generated. Omeprazole can heal underlying ulcers, but it does not relieve an established fixed obstruction, which usually needs endoscopic or surgical treatment. The retrieved literature is limited to case reports and descriptive articles, so this prediction is on Hold and is not evaluated further here.

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Examples from the first 20 listed:

  - **Moderate**: alprazolam, amphotericin B, amphetamine, atorvastatin, hydrochlorothiazide, fenofibrate, apalutamide, armodafinil.
  - **Minor**: lidocaine, nifedipine, acetylsalicylic acid.
- **Animal safety signal**: in rats, combining acid blockade with duodenogastric reflux promoted gastric carcinogenesis. Its relevance to humans is unknown.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only clinical trial is a diagnostic imaging study, and the human literature consists of small physiological studies. Omeprazole acts only indirectly on duodenogastric reflux, and rat studies suggest a possible carcinogenesis risk when acid suppression is combined with bile reflux. In the Philippines, the only registration is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled clinical trials of omeprazole in duodenogastric reflux, with symptom and bile exposure endpoints
- Long-term safety assessment of mucosal changes under acid suppression with bile reflux
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- The Philippines package insert, to review warnings and contraindications
- A decision on the registration status in the Philippines, since the only registration is cancelled
