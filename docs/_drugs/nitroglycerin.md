# Nitroglycerin: From an Unspecified Original Indication to Pulmonary Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Nitroglycerin is a nitric oxide (NO)-donating vasodilator. Its original approved indication is not recorded in the Philippine registration data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary hypertension**,
with **3 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Most of the evidence is small, short-term and hemodynamic.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, nitroglycerin is an NO donor. It releases nitric oxide, which activates soluble guanylate cyclase, raises cGMP and relaxes vascular smooth muscle. This pathway is directly relevant to lowering pulmonary vascular resistance.

Nitroglycerin is long established as a vasodilator for cardiovascular conditions, so the leap to pulmonary vascular disease is mechanistically coherent. Inhaled or nebulized delivery may give relative pulmonary selectivity and limit systemic hypotension. Human hemodynamic studies from 1983–84 and pediatric inhaled-nitroglycerin reports (2021, 2025) support a vasodilatory effect.

There are important caveats. The evidence is mostly small and short-term. No Phase 3 or randomized outcome data are present in the supplied material. In COPD-related pulmonary hypertension, vasodilators can worsen ventilation-perfusion matching, so benefit is not uniform across pulmonary hypertension groups.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text)**: hypotensive disorder, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, methemoglobinemia, sulfhemoglobinemia, anemia, macrocytic anemia, intracerebral hemorrhage, constrictive pericarditis, restrictive cardiomyopathy and cardiac tamponade. These should be confirmed against the official package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Examples from the first 20 reviewed:
  - Moderate: bupropion, morphine, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, dronabinol, nabilone, sapropterin, rosiglitazone.
  - Minor: acetylsalicylic acid, omeprazole, and several anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, scopolamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine, and others).
- **Clinical consideration**: systemic vasodilation can cause hypotension, and vasodilators may worsen ventilation-perfusion matching in chronic lung disease. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and small studies, including one randomized neonatal trial, suggest a pulmonary vasodilatory effect. However, there is no Phase 3 or confirmatory evidence, and the only Philippine registration is cancelled. The package insert safety data and the original indication are also missing. The other predicted indications are either already standard practice (Prinzmetal angina) or lack credible support (kyphoscoliotic heart disease, primary hereditary glaucoma, congenital hypotrichosis milia).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the DOH-FDA Philippines (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data and original indication confirmation
- A valid or re-registered product with a suitable inhaled/nebulized route, since route compatibility has not been assessed
- Larger randomized trials in defined pulmonary hypertension groups, with attention to V/Q mismatch risk in chronic lung disease
