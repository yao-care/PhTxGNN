## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, doxycycline is a broad-spectrum tetracycline antibiotic. Its efficacy against many bacterial infections, including *Chlamydia trachomatis*, is established. Mechanistically it may be applicable to ocular surface inflammation.

Chlamydial follicular conjunctivitis is a recognised cause of eye infection, and doxycycline is active against *Chlamydia trachomatis*. Doxycycline is also reported to have anti-inflammatory and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-inhibiting effects that could plausibly help ocular surface inflammation.

The only supporting paper describes persistent punctate keratitis appearing after chlamydial conjunctivitis had been treated with oral tetracycline or doxycycline. It does not test whether doxycycline treats punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis. In fact, the keratitis appeared or recurred after the antibiotic course. The link therefore rests mainly on the model's prediction and a general mechanistic argument, not on direct efficacy data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but there are no clinical trials. The only publication is a 1992 case report that does not evaluate doxycycline's efficacy for this condition. The evidence is at the L4 level. The only local registration is cancelled, so there is no currently valid marketed product in the Philippines.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct clinical evidence, such as controlled studies of doxycycline for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis or related ocular surface inflammation
- Mechanism of action data to support the MMP-inhibition and anti-inflammatory hypothesis
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Philippine FDA
- Clarification of the registration status, route of administration (oral or topical ocular), and available dosage forms in the Philippines
