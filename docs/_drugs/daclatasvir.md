## One-Sentence Summary

Daclatasvir is an NS5A inhibitor originally used to treat chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but the **4 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this prediction all concern HCV treatment or HBV reactivation, not HBV efficacy. The prediction is best read as a research question, not as supported repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, daclatasvir is a direct-acting antiviral that targets the HCV NS5A protein, and its efficacy in hepatitis C is well established.

HCV and HBV are both hepatotropic viruses, which may partly explain the high TxGNN score: the score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. However, the data show **no established anti-HBV mechanism** for daclatasvir. HCV is an RNA virus and HBV is a DNA virus that replicates through reverse transcription, and the NS5A target has no known counterpart in HBV.

The literature points the other way. Several case reports and cohorts describe **HBV reactivation** during daclatasvir-based therapy in HCV/HBV co-infected patients. This is a safety signal, not evidence of benefit.

**Evidence level note:** The automated scoring assigned L2 because one Phase 2/3 trial was completed. That trial (NCT02555943) enrolled only 23 patients and studied HBV reactivation during anti-HCV treatment, not HBV efficacy. Judged on whether any study tests daclatasvir against HBV, the evidence is closer to L5 (model prediction only). The table above reflects this.

  - Clarithromycin
  - Eluxadoline
  - Cobicistat

  Moderate-level interactions include metformin, loperamide, aprepitant, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, apixaban and rifaximin, among others.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- Daclatasvir has no known anti-HBV mechanism, and none of the compiled trials or publications tests HBV efficacy. The high TxGNN score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.
- The only HBV-related findings are reactivation events, which are a safety concern.
- The only Philippines registration is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and preclinical (in vitro) evidence of anti-HBV activity
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Philippines regulator
- A decision on whether to pursue this prediction at all, given that other predicted indications (HIV, hepatitis E, hepatitis A and others) also lack direct evidence
