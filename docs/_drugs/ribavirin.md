## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Ribavirin is a nucleoside analogue with broad antiviral activity. Its known actions include inhibition of the enzyme IMPDH, depletion of the cellular GTP pool, and immune modulation. It is established in hepatitis C and RSV infection.

Hepatitis B and hepatitis C are both chronic viral liver infections, which explains why the model places them close together. Mechanistically, though, the link is weak. Hepatitis B is a DNA virus that replicates through an RNA intermediate, so a direct antiviral effect of ribavirin is expected to be limited. Any benefit seen in trials is more plausibly immunomodulatory, as an add-on to peginterferon.

Most hepatitis B-related trials involve HCV/HBV co-infection or combination regimens, so ribavirin's own contribution is hard to isolate. Nucleos(t)ide analogues such as entecavir and tenofovir are the standard of care for hepatitis B, which also limits the clinical need for ribavirin.

## Safety Considerations

- **Relations recorded in the knowledge graph (not label text):** The knowledge graph links ribavirin with contraindication relations to several conditions. These include chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, asthma, sarcoidosis, interstitial lung disease, liver cirrhosis, impaired renal function, sickle cell anaemia and other haemoglobinopathies, haemolytic and other anaemias, pancytopenia, myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease and atherosclerosis, pancreatitis, periodontitis, and autoimmune hepatitis. These need verification against the package insert.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interacting drugs are recorded, but none has a severity level assigned. Examples include calcitriol, pantoprazole, glimepiride, mesalazine, doxycycline, metformin, omeprazole and prednisone.
- **Context-specific concern:** Haemolytic anaemia is a known concern with ribavirin, which matters for patients with cirrhosis or blood disorders.

Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Philippines are not available. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 3 trial (NCT00114361) tests peginterferon plus ribavirin directly in hepatitis B, and a Phase 2/3 pilot (NCT00275938) also studied it. The evidence level is therefore L2. However, neither isolates ribavirin's own contribution. The mechanistic rationale is weak, effective nucleos(t)ide analogues are the standard of care, and the Philippines safety label data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results of NCT00114361 and NCT00275938, specifically the effect of ribavirin relative to peginterferon alone
- Results of the ribavirin add-on pilot (NCT03759782) with nucleotide analogues
- Mechanism-of-action data
- Philippines package insert warnings and contraindications
- A risk assessment for haemolytic anaemia and the other contraindication signals listed above
