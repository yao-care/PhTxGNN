## One-Sentence Summary

Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite chemotherapy drug, originally used for acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and related leukemias.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukemia**.
The data compiled for this report list **15 clinical trials** and **20 publications** under this prediction, but most trials do not test mercaptopurine directly, so the direct evidence is weaker than the number suggests.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite. Its thioguanine nucleotide metabolites are incorporated into DNA and RNA and inhibit de novo purine synthesis. This should hit rapidly dividing leukemic blasts, whether lymphoid or myeloid.

ALL and myeloid leukemia are both acute leukemias of blood-forming cells. Mercaptopurine is already part of the established treatment for acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL), a myeloid subtype. APL maintenance classically combines mercaptopurine with methotrexate. Published reports also describe oral 6-MP plus methotrexate maintenance in transplant-ineligible AML, and ALL-like consolidation in pediatric AML.

Many of the listed Phase 3 trials are large leukemia protocols in which mercaptopurine is, at most, part of the chemotherapy backbone. Only one trial (NCT00003934) names mercaptopurine explicitly as a randomized maintenance component. The prediction is therefore plausible, but direct proof of benefit in myeloid leukemia is limited.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications / risk conditions (knowledge-graph-derived, not label text)**: kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, cholestasis (including benign recurrent and familial intrahepatic forms), hyperbilirubinemia (including hereditary forms and Gilbert syndrome), thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, anemia (including macrocytic), pyoureter, and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in total. Moderate-level ones include mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine, sulfasalazine and naltrexone. Levofloxacin is minor. Many others, such as pantoprazole, omeprazole, prednisone and metformin, have an unclassified severity level.
- **Pharmacogenomic and co-medication signals from the literature**: TPMT and NUDT15 variants predict myelosuppression. Allopurinol is reported to change 6-MP metabolism and toxicity. Thiopurine therapy in inflammatory bowel disease has been associated with lymphoproliferative disorders.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings. Label warnings are not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The biological rationale is sound, and mercaptopurine already has a role in APL maintenance. However, the Phase 3 trials listed mostly do not isolate mercaptopurine's contribution, and the supporting AML literature is small, observational or old.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of mercaptopurine's exact role in the key APL and AML protocols (NCT00003934, NCT00002701, NCT00492856, NCT00551460).
- Results from the ongoing AML combination studies (NCT06199557, NCT05506332).
- Detailed mechanism of action data and the package insert warnings and contraindications.
- Clarification of the cancelled registration status and a supply pathway in the Philippines.
- A safety plan covering TPMT/NUDT15 genotype-guided dosing, blood count and liver monitoring, and a drug interaction review.
