## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid, and drugs of this class reduce local inflammation and immune cell activity.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks the hair follicle, causing patchy, non-scarring hair loss. Suppressing the local immune response is therefore a plausible way to help. This fits the high TxGNN score.

There is an important caveat. The only direct controlled trial used hydrocortisone 1% cream as the weaker comparator against clobetasol propionate 0.05%. It therefore compares the two drugs and does not show that hydrocortisone works by itself. Reports from the 1950s to 1970s describe hair regrowth after intralesional hydrocortisone injection, but these were uncontrolled.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The single direct Phase 3 trial used hydrocortisone only as the weaker comparator, and the supporting reports are old and uncontrolled. The high model score is plausible mechanistically but is not yet backed by evidence that hydrocortisone itself works in alopecia areata.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled trial data on hydrocortisone as the investigational treatment for alopecia areata, against placebo or a standard topical steroid
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Philippine regulator
- Confirmation of the dosage form and route that would be used (topical or intralesional) against the locally registered product
