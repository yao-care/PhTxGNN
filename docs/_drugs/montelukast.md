## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is a cysteinyl leukotriene receptor antagonist.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bronchitis**.
The data compiled for this report include **17 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but most of them study bronchiolitis, not bronchitis in the strict sense. Only a few address bronchitis directly, mainly nonasthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis in children.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for montelukast are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on its known class, it blocks the cysteinyl leukotriene receptor (CysLT1). This may reduce airway inflammation, bronchoconstriction, mucus secretion and eosinophil-driven airway hyperreactivity.

These processes are also involved in post-viral airway disease and in some forms of bronchitis. Examples are eosinophilic bronchitis with cough and recurrent obstructive bronchitis in young children. A similar leukotriene-driven mechanism has been proposed for bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome after transplantation, supported by a rat transplant model (PMID 28545478).

The link is indirect. Many of the listed studies address infant viral bronchiolitis, post-bronchiolitis wheeze or transplant-related bronchiolitis obliterans. These are different diseases from bronchitis, so they support the idea only by analogy.

## Safety Considerations

Many other entries have an unknown severity rating, such as pantoprazole, omeprazole, metformin, glimepiride and morphine.

The local package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high (0.9995), and montelukast is marketed in the Philippines. However, most supporting studies address bronchiolitis, not bronchitis. Direct evidence is limited to a few small studies in eosinophilic and recurrent obstructive bronchitis, and the local package insert safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Philippine package insert warnings and contraindications.
- Full results of the direct bronchitis studies (NCT04613180, NCT01121016 and PMID 25563311), with confirmation in a larger randomised trial in a defined bronchitis population.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data.
- Review of the moderate-severity drug interactions, especially with corticosteroids and antimicrobials commonly used in respiratory care.
