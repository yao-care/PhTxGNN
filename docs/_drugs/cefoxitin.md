# Cefoxitin: From Bacterial Infections to Urinary Tract Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Cefoxitin is a second-generation cephamycin antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections such as pneumonia, peritonitis, gonorrhea and endometritis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **urinary tract infection**,
with **1 clinical trial** (terminated, 6 participants) and **about 20 publications** linked to this direction. The human evidence is mostly historical and low-powered.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, cefoxitin is a cephamycin beta-lactam antibiotic that is active against many Gram-positive, Gram-negative and anaerobic bacteria. Its efficacy in bacterial infections has been established, and it may mechanistically be applicable to urinary tract infection.

Cefoxitin has activity against Gram-negative bacteria, including *E. coli*, and is stable against many beta-lactamases, including some ESBLs. A mouse model (PMID 22214774) supports its use as a carbapenem-sparing option for urinary tract infection caused by ESBL-producing *E. coli*. Clinical studies from the 1970s also reported efficacy in complicated urinary tract infection.

Urinary tract infection is an extension within the existing antibacterial profile rather than true repurposing. The human evidence is historical, uncontrolled or low-powered, and the only registered trial gave no usable efficacy data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 35 interactions were identified. The main ones rated Moderate are:

  - Picosulfuric acid
  - Dicoumarol
  - Heparin is rated Minor.
  - Others, such as vancomycin, metronidazole, omeprazole and morphine, have an unknown severity level.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- Urinary tract infection has the best support among the predictions (L3), but the evidence is historical or preclinical. The only registered trial was terminated with 6 participants, and this is an extension within cefoxitin's existing antibacterial spectrum rather than true repurposing. The only Philippines registration is cancelled, and current practice favours other agents because of resistance.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Current local susceptibility data for urinary pathogens, including ESBL- and AmpC-producing organisms
- Controlled clinical evidence versus current standard therapy, since the one registered trial gave no efficacy data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from the Philippines regulator, and a decision on whether product re-registration is feasible
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Renal function and *C. difficile* risk management plan, given the knowledge-graph contraindication signals
