# Dorzolamide: From Open-Angle Glaucoma to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Dorzolamide is a topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, originally used to lower eye pressure in open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **primary hereditary glaucoma**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** specific to this indication were found, so the prediction rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Dorzolamide inhibits carbonic anhydrase in the ciliary processes of the eye. This reduces aqueous humor production and lowers intraocular pressure (IOP). The data compiled for this report list its pharmacological targets as carbonic anhydrase 1, 7, 12 and 14. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are otherwise not available.

Hereditary glaucoma is also a disease of raised eye pressure, so an IOP-lowering drug is mechanistically plausible. That plausibility comes from a shared treatment goal, not from any study in hereditary glaucoma. The high score is probably driven by the drug's established link to glaucoma in the knowledge graph.

Other high-scoring predictions for this drug include hair-loss conditions (hypotrichosis, alopecia), heart failure and respiratory conditions. No plausible mechanism or supporting evidence was found for any of these. The two highest-ranking glaucoma entries with real evidence (glaucoma 1 open angle, and open angle glaucoma) simply restate the approved use. They are not new repurposing opportunities.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for primary hereditary glaucoma.

For context, many completed Phase 3 and Phase 4 trials exist for dorzolamide in open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension, for example [NCT00878917](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00878917) (Phase 3, n=32) and [NCT00314171](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00314171) (Phase 3, n=437). These support the original indication only, not the predicted one.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from knowledge-graph drug-disease links, not label text):** diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, myasthenia gravis and other neuromuscular junction disease, hyperthyroidism (Graves disease, thyrotoxicosis), hypoglycemia, atrioventricular block, pulmonary emphysema, bronchial disease, anaphylaxis. Some of these likely reflect the beta-blocker component of dorzolamide/timolol combinations rather than dorzolamide alone, so they need confirmation against the product label.
- **Drug Interactions:** no drug-drug interactions were identified. The pharmacology records list only carbonic anhydrase targets (CA1, CA7, CA12, CA14).

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and precautions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for primary hereditary glaucoma has no supporting trials or literature (L5). The drug has no currently valid registration in the Philippines. Dorzolamide is well established for open-angle glaucoma, but that evidence does not extend to hereditary glaucoma.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical or preclinical evidence of IOP lowering specifically in primary hereditary glaucoma, such as pediatric or genetic glaucoma studies
- Confirmation of the current regulatory and market status in the Philippines
- The package insert, to confirm warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
