# Ketoconazole: From Superficial and Systemic Fungal Infections to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Ketoconazole is an azole antifungal originally used for fungal skin infections (such as tinea, cutaneous candidiasis and seborrheic dermatitis) and for some systemic mycoses.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but the support is still early: **1 clinical trial** (small, no results yet) and a handful of mostly indirect publications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ketoconazole is an imidazole antifungal that inhibits fungal CYP51 (lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase). Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this reasoning relies on general knowledge of the drug class.

There are three plausible links to acne:
- **Androgen effect:** At higher systemic doses, ketoconazole also inhibits human steroidogenic enzymes (CYP17A1, CYP11A1) and lowers androgen synthesis. This could reduce sebum production and androgen-driven acne, such as acne in PCOS-related hyperandrogenism.
- **Antibacterial effect:** Laboratory studies report that ketoconazole inhibits the growth and lipase activity of *Cutibacterium (Propionibacterium) acnes*, the bacterium involved in acne inflammation.
- **Malassezia link:** Topical ketoconazole acts against *Malassezia* (*Pityrosporum*) yeast. This may help *Malassezia* folliculitis, which is often misdiagnosed as acne, rather than true acne vulgaris.

The very high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and is not a substitute for clinical evidence. Systemic use for acne would also be hard to justify given the safety profile (see below).

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, liver disease, gallbladder disease, adrenocortical insufficiency, Addison disease, skin atrophy, telangiectasis, peripheral vascular disease and achlorhydria.
- **Drug interactions (100 recorded; examples):**
  - **Major:** loperamide, triamcinolone, budesonide
  - **Moderate:** famotidine, ranitidine, omeprazole, rabeprazole, hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, pioglitazone, aprepitant, alosetron, atropine, hyoscyamine
  - **Minor:** amphotericin B

Package insert warnings and contraindications for the Philippines were not available. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but the evidence is at the model-prediction and laboratory level (L4). The only trial is small, has no results, and its design (topical use) differs from the systemic mechanism most plausibly linked to acne. The Philippines registration is cancelled, and systemic ketoconazole carries significant liver, QT and adrenal concerns, which makes a systemic acne indication unattractive.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from NCT07237763 and further randomized trials of topical ketoconazole in confirmed acne vulgaris (distinguishing it from *Malassezia* folliculitis)
- Mechanism of action data and a clearer rationale for route (topical vs. systemic)
- Philippines package insert warnings and contraindications, and current registration or product availability for a topical formulation
- A safety assessment covering hepatotoxicity, QT prolongation, adrenal effects and drug interactions
